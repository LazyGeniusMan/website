After a semester of running ProcessMaker on a campus VPS hands-on, the next Business Process Management term at [FEB Unpad](https://feb.unpad.ac.id) moved me into supervision: guiding a teaching assistant on server administration and process automation implementation, and guiding students through their automation projects.

## From doing to supervising

The prior term built the playbook: budget a cost-effective VPS, run multi-instance ProcessMaker in Docker, keep student environments isolated. This term the job was handing that playbook over — reviewing the assistant's server work, unblocking student models, and keeping the classroom platform healthy through the semester.

## Projects over screenshots

Students shipped executable automation projects, not screenshots of one. Supervision meant checking the model against the process it claimed to enact, then checking the deployment against the model — the same model-to-running-system trace used in every later delivery engagement at [PT Navcore Nextology](/experience/navcore-2023).

## Teaching is handover

A course that depends on one admin fails the next term. Writing down the VPS, Docker, and ProcessMaker routines so an assistant could run them is the same operating-model instinct that later carried crowdfunding, banking, and food-delivery requirements from process to build without guessing.
